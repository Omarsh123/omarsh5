A study examining online shopping habits, platform preferences, and demographic patterns among 20 participants.

Introduction

This report summarizes the results of a university survey on online shopping habits among 20 participants. The goal is to identify patterns in e-commerce behavior across different age groups, genders, and education levels.

Analysis & Conclusions

Online Purchasing vs. Browsing Only

80% of respondents have made at least one online purchase. The remaining 20% browse but don't buy, citing payment security concerns and a preference for in-store shopping.

Purchase Frequency

Half of active buyers shop online once a month, while 31% shop weekly. Only 19% purchase as infrequently as once a year, suggesting online shopping is now a routine habit.

Age Range Distribution

Younger adults (18–39) make up 65% of respondents and are the most active buyers. However, all respondents aged 40 and above still reported making online purchases, showing broad adoption across age groups.

Gender Distribution

The sample is nearly balanced between female (50%) and male (45%) respondents. Female respondents leaned toward fashion platforms like Shein, while male respondents favored Amazon and eBay.

Education Level

College graduates are the most active online shoppers in this sample. High school respondents were more likely to browse without purchasing, possibly reflecting income differences.

Favorite Online Shopping Sites

Amazon dominated with 14 mentions. Temu and Shein performed strongly among younger respondents, while eBay, Walmart.com, and Target.com attracted smaller but consistent audiences.

Overall Conclusion

Online shopping is mainstream across all demographics in this sample. Amazon leads the market, but discount platforms like Temu and Shein are gaining ground fast among younger buyers. Higher education and younger age both correlate with more frequent online purchasing.
