Tools & Parts Reference

What our technicians use and when each tool matters.

Precision Screwdriver Set

Pentalobe (iPhone bottom screws), Phillips #000 (interior screws), Torx T3/T4 (Samsung), and tri-wing bits. Wrong bit = stripped head.

$8 – $25

Plastic Spudger Set

Non-conductive prying tools for lifting ribbon cables, releasing locking clips, and separating housings without scratching metal.

$3 – $10

Suction Cup Handle

Attaches to the display glass and applies steady lifting force while you insert plastic picks along the frame edge.

$2 – $8

Heat Gun / iOpener Pad

Softens the adhesive seal around the phone perimeter. A hair dryer works in a pinch, but a heat gun gives more precise, controllable temperature.

$15 – $50

ESD-Safe Tweezers

Anti-static tweezers for handling tiny screws, ribbon cable connectors, and motherboard components without causing static discharge damage.

$5 – $15

Plastic Opening Picks

Thin, flexible picks that slide around the phone frame to release adhesive. Place 5–6 picks simultaneously to hold your progress as you work.

$2 – $5

Magnetic Project Mat

Silicone mat with labeled zones for keeping screws in order. The magnetic surface prevents tiny screws from rolling off the workbench.

$6 – $18

B-7000 Adhesive

Semi-flexible, waterproof glue used to re-seal screens and back panels after repair. Full cure strength is reached after 24 hours.

$3 – $8

Multimeter

Tests charging ports, battery voltage, and short circuits on the motherboard. Essential for advanced troubleshooting and board-level repair.

$15 – $40

Compressed Air Can

Clears speaker grilles, charging ports, and camera lenses quickly without disassembly. Always the first step for audio and camera issues.

 Safety First

Tips & Warnings

Read this before attempting any fix — it protects your data, your hardware, and your safety.

Disconnect the Battery First

Always unplug the battery connector before touching any other component. A live battery can short-circuit, erase data, or cause a thermal event if probed accidentally.

Opening Voids Your Warranty

Disassembling the device typically voids the manufacturer warranty. If your phone is still covered, contact the manufacturer or carrier first — the repair may be free.

Ground Yourself Against Static

A single static discharge can permanently destroy logic board components. Touch a grounded metal surface or wear an ESD wrist strap before handling any PCB or cable.

Never Puncture a Battery

Lithium-ion batteries release toxic gases and can ignite if bent, pierced, or crushed. Use only plastic tools near the battery — never a metal spudger or screwdriver.

Photograph Every Step

Take photos at each stage before removing screws or cables. It is very easy to forget cable routing or which screw length belongs in which hole when reassembling.

Apply Heat in Short Bursts

Heat the frame for 30–60 seconds at a time only. Overheating can damage the OLED panel, degrade battery cells, or melt plastic clips. Keep temperature below 80 °C.

Back Up Before You Start

Back up to iCloud, Google Drive, or a computer before any repair. A repair can go wrong, and restoring from a backup is far easier than losing everything.

Keep Screws Organized

Screws are often different lengths. Mixing them up causes cracked housings or short circuits. Use a magnetic mat with labeled sections, or tape screws to paper with notes.
